Prologue & Chapter 1

The Mechanics of Artificial Scarcity & The 16-Year Trajectory

ISINGAPORE — In Singapore, purchasing an automobile does not grant you the right to drive it. Prospective owners must first bid for and win a Certificate of Entitlement (COE)—a paper license valid for exactly 10 years.

Under the government's strictly enforced zero net vehicle population growth cap, supply released at twice-monthly auctions is predetermined almost entirely by historical vehicle deregistrations. When supply is inelastic ($E_s pprox 0$), rising household wealth and speculative pressure translate directly into massive price inflation.

Chapter 2 & 3

The Scissors Curve & The 2023 Cut-and-Fill Policy Injection

The statistical correlation between annual car quota supply and Cat B clearing price is -0.469 (and -0.768 for Open Cat E). To arrest runaway inflation, the Ministry of Transport enacted the Cut-and-Fill mechanism in May 2023, redistributing over 30,800 future guaranteed deregistrations forward into the market.

Empirical Analysis

Why SingStat Isolates Motor Vehicles

Because an automobile in Singapore costs upwards of S$150,000 to S$300,000, vehicle purchases create enormous statistical swings in national retail sales data. In SingStat’s quarterly releases, headline retail figures fluctuate wildly depending on COE quota releases.

When we correlate SingStat's quarterly `Motor Vehicles` retail sales index against Cat B COE premiums across 63 consecutive quarters (2010 to 2025), we discover a striking empirical divergence:

  • The Motor Volume Depression ($r = -0.387$): When COE prices peak, dealership transaction volume falls because fewer quotas are physically issued. Automotive sales volume shrinks during price booms.
  • The Wealth Decoupling ($r = +0.745$): Meanwhile, `Total (Excluding Motor Vehicles)` shows a strong positive correlation with COE prices, proving that COE price spikes are driven by macro-liquidity and affluence even as car dealership unit volumes contract.

Empirical Analysis

How Housing Windfalls Financed Automotive Auctions

Why did COE premiums cross S$150,000 despite high 4%–5% car loan interest rates and strict Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) borrowing regulations?

Under MAS rules, vehicles with an Open Market Value (OMV) over $20,000 require a minimum 40% cash down payment and carry a maximum loan tenure of 7 years. For a typical S$250,000 mid-tier car, a buyer must produce at least S$100,000 to S$120,000 in liquid cash.

Our analysis reveals that vehicle bidding has decoupled from wage growth and is now sustained by realized public housing equity. Mature-estate HDB owners and private property downgraders who realized S$300,000 to S$600,000 in capital windfalls possessed the unencumbered cash required to absorb high vehicle downpayments without borrowing distress.
